### 3. Social dynamics under the neon glow  

1. **Leadership and followership** – The two women who are turned away from the camera seem to occupy “anchor points” in the flow. Their orientation away from the street suggests they are focused on something internal (a phone, a conversation). 2. **Groups vs. isolates** – Small clusters (pairs or trios) move together toward the bars, their trajectories tightly coupled. The proximity sensor registers synchronized gait cycles, a classic sign of *social facilitation*: individuals tend to match each other’s speed when in a shared goal space. Solitary pedestrians, however, weave more erratically, occasionally pausing at a neon sign as if to “read” the environment. Could these lone figures be *boundary‑testers*, probing the limits of the crowd’s flow? Or are they simply tourists, unfamiliar with the rhythm of Bourbon Street?

Establishments with brighter, larger signs tend to become *attractors* in a crowd‑dynamics model, pulling more people into their immediate vicinity. Yet the *actual* power may lie elsewhere: a bartender inside the “Krazy Korner” can dictate who enters, who stays, and who is turned away. My external view cannot capture the interior negotiations, reminding me that visible dominance (bright lights) does not always equal social control.
