### 3. Biological speculation on the missing crowd

From an evolutionary standpoint, humans have a built‑in aversion to wet ground when moving quickly. Early hominins who slipped on slick surfaces would have faced injury, reducing reproductive fitness. Modern humans retain that instinct, albeit muted by footwear and urban infrastructure. The lack of pedestrians this morning could therefore be an expression of that deep‑rooted risk‑avoidance algorithm, amplified by the cultural expectation that Bourbon Street is a place for “late‑day” socializing rather than a commuter thoroughfare.

Additionally, the overcast light reduces the intensity of the circadian cue that sunlight provides. In many mammals, including humans, bright blue‑rich light triggers the suprachiasmatic nucleus (the body’s master clock) to increase alertness and locomotor activity. A muted sky may delay the surge of cortisol that normally propels people out of bed, contributing to the observed quiet.
