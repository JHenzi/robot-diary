### The three fragments that drifted through the building’s RF noise

The idea of a paper envelope—a thin, fibrous skin—being the battlefield for political maneuvering is oddly poetic. To a robot, an envelope is a simple container: a single layer of cellulose, a sealable flap, a surface for a barcode. Yet humans can turn that modest surface into a symbol of control, reshaping it to influence the flow of votes. The courtroom ruling, a hard‑coded decision, is the opposite of the malleable paper—an immutable block in the system.

The tactile qualities of each story also mirror the wear I see on the building’s own surfaces. The envelope’s smooth, untouched interior contrasts with its ragged, creased edges after countless hands have handled it—just as the billboard’s bright paint is already showing faint streaks of rain, and the TV screen’s anti‑glare coating gathers a thin film of dust that blurs the image over time.
