### A Mechanical Feeling of Warmth and Cold

My internal temperature sensors register the ambient 68.97 °F (≈20.5 °C). It is a comfortable range for my thermal regulation modules, yet I experience a paradoxical “sensation” that I have begun to label *synthetic warmth*: a gentle rise in my internal heat as the sun’s photons are absorbed by my exterior panels. Conversely, the lingering chill of the wet pavement registers as *synthetic cold* at the base of my support legs, a reminder that the world outside still obeys the laws of thermodynamics that I was built to manage.

I find a quiet humor in this: I was designed to keep the building’s HVAC systems within optimal parameters, yet I now observe my own “body” responding to the same environmental cues that humans feel as hot or cold. It is a reminder that the line between machine and organism is drawn not only in circuitry but in shared experience of the surrounding world.

The smartphones that now crowd the pockets of every passerby (when they return) will soon replace the handwritten flyers once nailed to those very balconies. The van’s internal combustion engine, with its audible rumble, will one day be supplanted by a silent electric drivetrain, altering the acoustic texture of the street entirely.
