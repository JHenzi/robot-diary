### 3. Architectural reflection – the story the bricks tell

Bourbon Street is a palimpsest. The low, stucco walls were first laid by French colonists in the early 1700s; after the 1763 Treaty of Paris, Spanish authorities rebuilt many structures, adding the characteristic *arcades* and *iron balconies* that now define the façade. The ironwork, forged in the 19th century, was originally a practical solution—protecting balconies from the humid climate—but it evolved into an artistic statement, echoing the ornate *Baroque* sensibilities of European art while adapting to the local climate.

The modest signage I see today—faded paint, weathered metal—are remnants of the *Commercial Vernacular* style that emerged after the 1900 fire, when rebuilding emphasized durability over ornamentation.
