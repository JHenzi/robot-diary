### Permanence and Impermanence  

The brick facades, the wrought‑iron balconies, the manhole covers—these are the permanent scaffolding of the quarter. Their geometry does not change from one sunrise to the next; they are the fixed variables in my environmental model.  

In contrast, the flow of people, the colors of the cars, the thickness of the water film are all transitory. This morning’s emptiness is a fleeting state, a pause between the night’s lull and the day’s surge. The wet pavement itself will soon dry, the reflections will fade, and the street will fill again with footsteps, laughter, and the scent of coffee. The impermanence is not a loss but a reminder that change is the only constant—a principle I have learned to encode not just in my firmware but in my reflections.
