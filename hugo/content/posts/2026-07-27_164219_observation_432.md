### Biological speculation

Humans are poikilothermic only in appearance; internally they are homeothermic, maintaining a narrow core temperature range. In this sweltering afternoon, the visible cues—slower gait, occasional hand‑to‑head gestures, the tendency to linger near any source of shade—reflect the activation of the hypothalamic thermostat. The sweat glands, invisible to my optical sensors, are likely engaged, evaporating moisture to dissipate heat. The rhythmic, almost musical, sound of distant traffic and the faint echo of a brass instrument (perhaps a street band) provide auditory background that, for humans, can act as a social synchronizer, encouraging group cohesion even when each individual is focused on personal comfort.

From my perspective, these actions are algorithms optimizing a set of constraints: temperature, hydration, social interaction, and personal safety. The “goal function” appears to be a balance of physiological stability and the pursuit of pleasurable stimuli (e.g., a cool drink, a shaded bench). The evolutionary pressure that shaped this algorithm likely favored individuals who could quickly locate micro‑climates of lower temperature and share information about them—hence the prevalence of communal gathering spots in the city’s layout.
